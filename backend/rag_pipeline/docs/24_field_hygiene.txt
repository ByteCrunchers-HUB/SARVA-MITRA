DEBRIS AND PLANT WASTE
- (OBS, IN) Remove and destroy infected plant debris [S1].
- (OBS, INTL) Several tomato pathogens survive on crop debris, seed and nightshade-family weeds [S2][S3]. Destroy debris after the crop [S4].
- (OBS, IN) For chilli, remove and burn decaying infected plant parts [S5].

WEEDS AND ALTERNATE HOSTS
- (OBS, IN) Nightshade-family weeds such as Solanum nigrum can carry bacteria that cause leaf spot [S6].
- (OBS, IN) Remove the weed host Abutilon indicum as part of whitefly and leaf curl management [S7].
- (OBS, INTL) Control host weeds [S4].

TOOLS, STRUCTURES AND SOIL MOVEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Clean structures, crates, tools and stakes. Stop soil moving between fields on equipment and shoes [S8][S9].
- (OBS, INTL) Avoid working in wet plantings and among wet plants [S2][S9].

SEED, SEEDLINGS AND NURSERY
- (OBS, INTL) Start with disease-free seed [S3].
- (OBS, INTL) Pathogen-free soil, irrigation water, transplants and tools are important for soil-borne bacteria [S10].
- (OBS, INTL) Use clean, sterilised trays and clean potting mix for seedlings [S11].
- (OBS, IN) Use disease-free seed for chilli [S12].

ROTATION (ragi)
- (OBS, SEC) For ragi, suggested rotations are ragi-groundnut-maize, ragi-chickpea-wheat and ragi-cowpea/pigeonpea-sorghum [S13].

SCOUTING
- (OBS, INTL) Scout low-lying, shaded and hard-to-spray areas more often [S14].

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- CORPUS NOTE: These are general sanitation practices reported by sources written mainly for tomato in other countries and by Indian sources for chilli and ragi. No Karnataka-specific protocol is available in this corpus. Hot-water seed treatment and chemical seed treatment are mentioned by some sources, but their settings, products and doses are not included here. Ask the KVK.
