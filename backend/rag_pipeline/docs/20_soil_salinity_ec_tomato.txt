DOC_ID: 20_soil_salinity_ec_tomato
TITLE: Soil salinity (electrical conductivity) and tomato yield
CROPS: tomato
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_ec_dsm (dS/m), especially above about 2.5 dS/m or a rising trend; irrigation water EC (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (PHYS, INTL) Salt stress is a physiological problem, not an infection. Every crop tolerates some salt in the root zone. The highest level with no yield loss is called the salinity threshold, and above it yield falls in proportion to salt level [S1][S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Soil salinity is expressed as the electrical conductivity of a saturated soil extract (ECe) in dS/m, the same as mmhos/cm [S1][S2].

THRESHOLDS (tomato)
- (PHYS, INTL, USA) The tomato salinity threshold is 2.5 dS/m (ECe), with about 9.9% yield loss per dS/m above it (classic Maas-Hoffman values) [S2][S3].
- (WEAK, INTL, Egypt and others) Other studies report thresholds of about 2.97 and 3.31 dS/m, with slopes of 11-15% yield loss per dS/m [S3].
- (PHYS, INTL) Tomato is moderately sensitive. Growth and yield begin to decline when nutrient-solution EC exceeds about 2.5-4.0 dS/m. Moderate salinity can improve fruit sugar quality while lowering yield [S4].
- (PHYS, INTL, Egypt) Irrigation water up to 2.5 dS/m did not have major harmful effects on marketable yield in one study. Potassium fertiliser helped reduce the harm and blossom-end rot [S3].
- (PHYS, INTL, drip) In drip irrigation, water of 4.5 dS/m cut yield by about 10-12% compared with 1.2 dS/m [S5]. In a three-year study, keeping irrigation-water salinity under 4 dS/m and soil matric potential above -20 kPa helped maintain yield; BER rose at 3-4 dS/m, and 5.5 dS/m cut yield by roughly 12-31% [S6].
- (PHYS, INTL) Salinity reduces leaf area and chlorophyll at high levels [S6]. Salinity is linked to blossom-end rot (see 18_blossom_end_rot_tomato) [S6].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL, Egypt) Drip irrigation with careful management was recommended when saline water must be used, since fruit yield per unit of water was higher [S7].

CORPUS NOTE (not a source statement): Laboratory values are ECe on a saturated paste. A field sensor reading depends on soil moisture and sensor type, so soil_ec_dsm should be treated as a trend indicator and confirmed with a laboratory soil test.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No salinity thresholds for chilli or ragi, and no Karnataka-specific thresholds, are present in this corpus.
- CORPUS RULE: A high or rising soil_ec_dsm shows salinity risk. Salt damage is not a disease, and a sensor reading is not a diagnosis of it.
