TITLE: Heat stress, flower drop and poor fruit set in chilli
CROPS: chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature_c (day and night); humidity_pct
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: No chilli heat stress document existed in the previous corpus. This document is new. Tomato heat stress is in 19_heat_stress_fruit_set_tomato.txt. Ragi heat stress thresholds are not available.

IDENTIFICATION
- (PHYS, INTL) Heat stress is a physiological problem. High temperature reduces pollen viability and fruit set, and causes flower or bud drop in chilli [S1][S2]. It is not an infection.

TEMPERATURE FACTS
- (PHYS, INTL, review) Optimum temperature for chilli growth and fruit set is 25-30°C by day and 18-21°C at night. Above 32°C day temperature and/or 24°C night temperature, fruit set declines sharply through reduced pollen viability [S1][S2].
- (PHYS, INTL, USA) Night temperature may matter more than day temperature. Pollen germination and tube growth fall above 30°C day / 21°C night [S3].
- (PHYS, INTL, India) TNAU notes that high temperature (above 35°C) with low humidity causes flower drop and poor fruit set in chilli [S4].
- (PHYS, IN) A study in Punjab found maximum fruit set at 25-30°C day / 18-21°C night; above 35°C day, fruit set dropped to less than 20% [S5].
- (PHYS, INTL) Sustained high temperature (>32°C day) combined with low humidity (<50%) causes bud and flower abscission [S2].
- (PHYS, INTL) High humidity (>80%) for prolonged periods can also hinder pollen shedding in chilli, similar to tomato [S3].
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka-measured heat stress thresholds for chilli are present in this corpus. All values above are from international reviews or Indian (Punjab, TNAU) studies, not Karnataka-specific.

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, INTL) Look for flower or bud drop and for fruit that does not set [S2][S3].
- (INSP, INTL) Check for small, misshapen or parthenocarpic fruit (seedless) which can result from heat stress [S2].
- (INSP, IN) Leaves may curl upward (physiological leaf curl) under high temperature and moisture stress, distinct from viral leaf curl [S4].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Heat-tolerant varieties set fruit under hot conditions; keep notes on variety performance [S1][S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Provide shade net (30-50%) during peak summer if feasible [S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Maintain adequate soil moisture through drip irrigation; avoid water stress which compounds heat stress [S1].
- (OBS, INTL) Fruit-set hormone sprays can give puffy or malformed fruit; no growth regulator makes normal fruit under high heat [S3]. No product advice is given here.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka or ICAR chilli-specific heat stress thresholds in this corpus. Values are from reviews, USA extension, and Punjab/TNAU studies.
- CORPUS RULE: A hot reading shows heat-stress risk. Flower drop and fruit set problems must be checked in the field. Heat stress is not a disease and a hot reading must not be reported as one.
