DOC_ID: 09_bacterial_wilt_ralstonia
TITLE: Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum) - hot, wet soil conditions that favour it
CROPS: tomato; chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct (high for days); temperature_c; rainfall_mm; irrigation events (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, INTL) Bacterial wilt is caused by Ralstonia solanacearum. It is one of the most devastating tomato diseases in tropical and subtropical regions; the bacterium lives in soil and has a very wide host range [S1].
- (OBS, INTL) Infested soil and surface or irrigation water are the main sources. The bacterium enters roots, usually through wounds [S2].

WEATHER AND SOIL FACTS
- (FAV, INTL, USA) High temperature (about 29-35°C) and high soil moisture favour the disease [S2].
- (FAV, INTL, Japan, lab) In a laboratory study, wilt was lower in drier soil (20-30% of maximum water-holding capacity), and the pathogen survived less in dry soil [S3]. CORPUS NOTE: this unit is % of maximum water-holding capacity. It is not the same as the sensor column soil_moisture_pct and must not be used as a sensor threshold.
- (FAV, INTL, China) A field study found disease outcome was positively linked to soil moisture, and suggested that managing soil moisture could reduce losses [S4].
- (FAV, INTL, China) Warm months increase incidence. In a China field study, shifting transplanting time to avoid the hottest period reduced wilt [S1][S5].
- (WEAK, INTL) Higher temperature raised wilt severity in some resistant tomato varieties [S5][S6]. Cold-water irrigation reduced wilt in a pot study only [S7]; this is not field-proven and is not advice.
- (OBS, INTL) Chemical control is often ineffective because the bacteria sit deep in soil [S6]. The UF/IFAS sheet states no known resistance in commercial cultivars (USA context) [S2]; research on temperature-stable resistance genes is ongoing [S6].

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, IN) Rapid and complete wilting of normal-looking, fully grown plants [S8].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, IN) Note whether wilting is sudden and complete on otherwise green plants [S8].
- CORPUS NOTE: Wilting can have other causes (for example Fusarium wilt, see 10_fusarium_wilt_tomato). Sensor data cannot separate them; the KVK or agriculture officer must confirm.

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Pathogen-free soil, irrigation water, transplants and tools are important [S2].
- (WEAK, INTL) Managing soil moisture and planting date may reduce loss [S4][S5].

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka-measured threshold is present in this corpus. Temperature figures come from a USA extension sheet; soil moisture evidence comes from laboratory and Chinese field studies.
- CORPUS RULE: High soil moisture with high temperature shows conditions that favour bacterial wilt. It does not confirm it.
