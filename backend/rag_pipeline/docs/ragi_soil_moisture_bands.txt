TITLE: Ragi (finger millet) soil moisture - field-relevant bands and crop-stage guidance
CROPS: ragi (finger millet)
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct; temperature_c; rainfall_mm; irrigation events (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: This document summarizes field-relevant soil moisture information for ragi. It does NOT provide a universal soil_moisture_pct threshold. Ragi is predominantly rainfed (kharif); irrigation is rare. The sensor column soil_moisture_pct uses different units than research sources (per cent available soil moisture - PASM, % field capacity, kPa tension). Always confirm units before comparing.

CORPUS RULE: No universal soil moisture target exists. Ask the team for crop and soil-type targets, or a local KVK recommendation.

FIELD-RELEVANT CONTEXT FROM SOURCES

- (OBS, IN, Karnataka) UAS GKVK Bengaluru long-term study (2014-19) on finger millet: per cent available soil moisture (PASM) and yield. At 58% PASM, yield was ~50% of normal; at 40% PASM, yield was ~25% of normal [S1]. This is the most Karnataka-relevant soil moisture finding for ragi in this corpus.
- (OBS, IN) Finger millet is about 90% kharif rainfed; prefers loamy to sandy soil with good drainage [S2].
- (OBS, IN) Karnataka has the largest ragi area, with significant production in Bengaluru, Kolar, Tumakuru, Chitradurga, Hassan districts [S3].
- (OBS, IN) ICAR-IIMR: seedling diseases (root rot, damping-off) appear at seedling stage; blast is the major disease [S4].
- (OBS, IN) Rainfall variability impact study (Karnataka): finger millet showed moisture stress in deficit years (2016, 2018) across Karnataka [S5].
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka study directly measuring soil_moisture_pct sensor units for ragi was found. The UAS GKVK study used PASM (per cent available soil moisture), which is not the same as the sensor unit.

CROP STAGE GUIDANCE (rainfed kharif context)

Sowing to establishment (July - early August):
- Sowing window depends on monsoon onset; UAS Bengaluru tables define sowing windows [S6][S7].
- Adequate soil moisture at sowing is critical for germination [S2].
- Seedling damping-off (11) favoured by cool, wet, poorly drained soil [S8].

Vegetative / tillering (August - September):
- Rainfed; soil moisture depends on monsoon.
- Blast (15, ragi_blast_detailed) favoured by high humidity, cloudy, 25-28°C - not directly soil moisture but canopy humidity [S9].

Flowering to grain filling (September - October):
- CRITICAL PERIOD for yield. Moisture stress at this stage severely reduces grain fill [S1][S5].
- Neck blast and finger blast (ragi_blast_detailed) occur during this period; favoured by high humidity/cloudy weather [S9].

Maturity to harvest (October - November):
- Dry weather needed for harvest.
- High soil moisture/humidity at maturity can cause grain mold (17_ragi_other) [S2].

SENSOR READING INTERPRETATION HINTS

- LOW soil_moisture_pct sustained during flowering/grain fill (Sept-Oct) → moisture stress, yield loss likely (UAS GKVK: 40% PASM = 25% yield) [S1].
- HIGH soil_moisture_pct + high humidity_pct + cloudy (low sunlight_wm2) during flowering → conditions favour blast (ragi_blast_detailed, 15) [S9].
- VERY HIGH soil_moisture_pct + poor drainage reported at seedling stage → conditions favour damping-off/root rot (11, 17_ragi_other) [S8].
- SWINGING soil_moisture_pct (dry-wet cycles) during grain fill → may affect grain quality, but no specific ragi study in corpus.
- CORPUS RULE: These are conditions that favour problems. They do not confirm any disease or disorder. Field inspection is required.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No soil_moisture_pct sensor bands for ragi. The UAS GKVK PASM values (40%, 58%) are the closest but use different units.
- The UAS GKVK study is the only Karnataka soil moisture study for ragi in this corpus.
- Ragi is predominantly rainfed; irrigation-based soil moisture management is not typical.
