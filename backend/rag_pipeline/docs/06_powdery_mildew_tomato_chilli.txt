DOC_ID: 06_powdery_mildew_tomato_chilli
TITLE: Powdery mildew of tomato and chilli (Leveillula taurica and Oidium species) - conditions that favour it
CROPS: tomato; chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature_c; humidity_pct; leaf_wet (not required, free water harms spores); rainfall_mm; sunlight_wm2 (shade or low light); canopy density (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, IN) For chilli, TNAU and NIPHM name Leveillula taurica as the powdery mildew fungus [S7].
- (OBS, INTL) For tomato, sources describe powdery mildew from Leveillula and Oidium species [S2][S5].

WEATHER FACTS
- (FAV, INTL, USA) It does not need standing water on leaves. High humidity with moderate temperature of about 16-25°C favours it, and hot temperature with low humidity limits it [S1].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) It thrives in warm, dry weather with moderate to high humidity and can be suppressed by rain. Optimum is 15-27°C, and infection occurs below 30°C [S2].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Free moisture kills spores and inhibits germination. Moderate temperature and shade favour it. Spores are sensitive to extreme heat (above about 32°C) and direct sunlight [S3][S4].
- (FAV, INTL, Pacific region) Germination and infection occur at temperature under 30°C and at medium to high humidity, commonly in warm, dry weather [S5].
- (FAV, INTL, USA greenhouse) In greenhouses the optimum is 20-30°C, humidity above 95% and fairly low light [S6].
- (FAV, IN) For chilli, TNAU/NIPHM state that cool dry weather favours spore germination and high relative humidity favours disease development [S7]. A TNAU weather advisory (Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu) notes high humidity leading to powdery mildew in chilli [S8].
- (WEAK, INTL, IN) Sources differ: the international sources describe moderate-to-warm temperature, while the Indian chilli source describes cool dry weather for spore germination. No Karnataka threshold is available [S1][S7].

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, INTL, IN) Pale yellow blotches on the upper leaf surface, with white powdery growth mostly on the lower surface (chilli and tomato Leveillula) [S5][S7].
- (INSP, INTL) Leaves may yellow, die and drop; fruit can get sunscald [S4][S5]. It seldom kills plants but can sharply cut yield [S1].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, INTL, IN) Turn leaves over and check the lower surface for white powdery growth, and the upper surface for pale yellow blotches [S5][S7].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Avoid too much nitrogen because lush growth favours the disease. Remove volunteer plants. Use resistant varieties where available [S5].
- (OBS, INTL) Space plants about 24 inches (about 61 cm) or more apart for air flow, and avoid shady low fields [S2].
- (OBS, INTL, IN) Sources name chemical and biological options. Product names, doses and timing are not included here. Ask the KVK for approved products [S1][S7].

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No numeric Karnataka threshold for powdery mildew is present in this corpus.
- CORPUS RULE: Matching sensor readings show that conditions favour powdery mildew. They do not confirm it. A dry-leaf, humid reading (leaf_wet = 0) is consistent with this disease only as a risk signal.
