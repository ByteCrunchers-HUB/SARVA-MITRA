TITLE: Tomato leaf curl virus spread by whitefly
CROPS: tomato; chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: none reliable. The sources give no sensor threshold. Use only when the farmer reports curling leaves.
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, IN) The virus is carried by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci. Primary spread is by whiteflies and secondary spread is by movement of infected plants [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Whitefly damage on its own includes chlorotic spots, yellowing, and downward curling and drying of leaves [S2].
- (WEAK, INTL) A research paper states that vector activity, virus multiplication and symptom development are affected by weather during and after infection, but gives no thresholds [S3].
- (OBS, IN) A TNAU advisory also reports leaf curl in chilli [S4].

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, IN) Severe stunting, downward rolling and crinkling of leaves, older leaves leathery and brittle, short internodes, and a bushy look with more side branches [S1].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, IN) Look for whiteflies on the plants and for the curling and stunting described above [S1][S2].
- CORPUS NOTE: Whitefly feeding alone can also curl leaves [S2]. Field inspection and KVK confirmation are needed to tell virus from whitefly damage.

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, IN) TNAU lists: uproot and destroy leaf-curl plants, use nitrogen and irrigation judiciously, remove the weed host Abutilon indicum, and use yellow sticky traps [S2].
- (OBS, IN) A TNAU advisory for chilli leaf curl also recommends yellow sticky traps and names insecticide options [S4]. Trap numbers, products, doses and timing are not included here. Ask the TNAU or Karnataka KVK for local, approved recommendations.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No reliable sensor threshold exists in the retrieved sources for this disease. Sensor readings must not be used to suggest it.
- CORPUS RULE: A virus cannot be confirmed from sensor readings.
