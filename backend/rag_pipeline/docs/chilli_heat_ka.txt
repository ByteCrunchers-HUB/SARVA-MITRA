TITLE: Chilli heat stress - Karnataka verified thresholds for flower and fruit set
CROPS: chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature_c (day and night); humidity_pct; sunlight_wm2
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: This document replaces chilli_heat_stress.txt with Karnataka-specific data from UAS/UHS/IIHR sources. Tomato heat stress is in 19_heat_stress_fruit_set_tomato.txt. Ragi heat stress thresholds are not available in Karnataka sources.

CORPUS RULE: Sensors show conditions that favour a problem. They never confirm it. Heat stress is a physiological disorder, not a disease. A hot reading must not be reported as a disease.

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, KA) Chilli is grown in three seasons in Karnataka: kharif (Jun-Oct), rabi (Oct-Feb), and summer (Feb-May). Summer season faces highest heat stress risk [S1].
- (OBS, KA) Major chilli growing districts: Bengaluru Rural, Kolar, Chikkaballapur, Hassan, Chitradurga, Mysuru, Belagavi, Dharwad [S1].
- (OBS, KA) UAS Bengaluru, UHS Bagalkote, and IIHR Bengaluru have studied heat stress in Karnataka chilli varieties [S1][S2][S3].

KARNATAKA HEAT STRESS THRESHOLDS (UAS/UHS/IIHR)

Optimum Temperature Range for Chilli (Karnataka varieties):
- (PHYS, KA) IIHR Bengaluru: Day 25-30°C / Night 18-21°C for optimal growth and fruit set in Byadgi, Guntur, and local Karnataka varieties [S2].
- (PHYS, KA) UHS Bagalkote: Maximum fruit set at 28°C day / 20°C night; significant reduction above 32°C day / 24°C night [S3].
- (PHYS, KA) UAS Bengaluru: Pollen viability declines sharply above 33°C day temperature; night temperature above 25°C disrupts pollen tube growth [S1].

Critical Thresholds (Flower Drop & Fruit Set Failure):
- (PHYS, KA) Day temperature >34°C sustained for >3 days: >50% flower drop in sensitive varieties (Byadgi, Guntur) [S2].
- (PHYS, KA) Night temperature >26°C: Pollen tube growth inhibition, poor fruit set even if day temp is moderate [S1][S2].
- (PHYS, KA) Day >35°C + Night >27°C combined: Near-total flower abortion; parthenocarpic fruit may form [S3].
- (PHYS, KA) Daily mean temperature >30°C for >5 consecutive days: Significant yield reduction [S1].

Humidity Interaction:
- (PHYS, KA) Low humidity (<40%) + high temperature (>32°C): Accelerates flower desiccation and drop [S2].
- (PHYS, KA) High humidity (>80%) + high temperature (>30°C): Hinders pollen dehiscence and shedding [S1][S2].
- (PHYS, KA) Optimal humidity for fruit set: 60-75% at flowering [S2].

Variety-Specific Tolerance (Karnataka):
- (OBS, KA) Byadgi (dry chilli): Moderate heat tolerance; fruit set declines above 33°C day [S2].
- (OBS, KA) Guntur (hot chilli): Slightly better tolerance; fruit set declines above 34°C day [S2].
- (OBS, KA) Arka Lohit (IIHR release): Bred for heat tolerance; maintains fruit set up to 35°C day / 26°C night [S2][S3].
- (OBS, KA) Arka Abhir (IIHR release): Heat tolerant; suitable for summer cultivation in Karnataka [S3].
- (OBS, KA) Local landraces (Hassan, Chitradurga): Variable tolerance; generally adapted to local microclimate [S1].

SEASONAL RISK PROFILE (KARNATAKA)

Kharif (Jun-Oct): Low heat stress risk; temperatures generally within optimum [S1].
Rabi (Oct-Feb): Low to moderate risk; February can reach >32°C in North Karnataka [S1].
Summer (Feb-May): HIGH risk; April-May day temps 35-40°C common in Bengaluru, Kolar, Chitradurga, North Karnataka [S1].

FIELD INSPECTION - WHAT TO LOOK FOR TODAY
- (INSP, KA) Flower/bud drop: Check for abscised flowers on ground; count retained vs dropped flowers [S2].
- (INSP, KA) Poor fruit set: Flowers open but no fruit formation after 7-10 days [S2].
- (INSP, KA) Parthenocarpic fruit: Small, seedless, misshapen fruit (heat-induced) [S2][S3].
- (INSP, KA) Sunscald on fruit: White/papery patches on exposed fruit shoulders [S2].
- (INSP, KA) Physiological leaf curl: Upward curling, thickened, leathery leaves (distinct from viral curl) [S4].
- (INSP, KA) Pollen viability test (if feasible): Tap flowers on dark paper; viable pollen appears as yellow dust [S2].

MANAGEMENT (CULTURAL PRACTICES ONLY)
- (OBS, KA) Variety selection: Use heat-tolerant varieties (Arka Lohit, Arka Abhir) for summer planting [S2][S3].
- (OBS, KA) Planting time: Avoid flowering during peak summer (April-May); adjust transplanting to Feb for summer crop [S1].
- (OBS, KA) Shade net: 30-50% green shade net reduces canopy temperature by 2-4°C; feasible for high-value chilli [S2].
- (OBS, KA) Irrigation: Maintain adequate soil moisture (see chilli_soil_moisture_ka); water stress compounds heat stress [S1][S3].
- (OBS, KA) Mulching: Straw/mulch reduces soil temperature and conserves moisture [S1].
- (OBS, KA) Nutrient management: Avoid excess nitrogen during heat; potassium (K) improves heat tolerance [S2].
- (OBS, KA) Foliar sprays: Kaolin clay or antitranspirants may reduce leaf temperature; consult KVK [S3].

SENSOR READING INTERPRETATION (KARNATAKA CONTEXT)

Day temperature_c >34°C for 3+ consecutive readings + Night temperature_c >26°C:
→ HIGH heat stress risk; expect significant flower drop and fruit set failure [S1][S2].

Day temperature_c >35°C + Night temperature_c >27°C + Low humidity_pct <40%:
→ SEVERE heat stress risk; near-total flower abortion likely [S3].

Day temperature_c 32-34°C + High humidity_pct >80%:
→ MODERATE heat stress risk; pollen shedding hindered, poor fruit set [S1][S2].

Daily mean temperature_c >30°C for 5+ consecutive days:
→ Yield reduction risk; monitor flower retention [S1].

Morning temperature_c >25°C (indicating high night temps):
→ Early warning for night-temperature-induced pollen tube inhibition [S1][S2].

CORPUS RULE: These are conditions that favour heat stress. They do not confirm it. Field inspection of flower retention and fruit set is required. Heat stress is not a disease.
