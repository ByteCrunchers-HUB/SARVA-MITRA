TITLE: Early blight (Alternaria solani) - warm weather with alternating wet and dry periods
CROPS: tomato
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature_c; humidity_pct; leaf_wet (alternating wet and dry); rainfall_mm; plant stress or age (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: The ID keeps "potato" because it is an existing ID. This document is used for tomato only. Lines marked POTATO-DERIVED come from potato guides. No chilli-specific early blight source is in this corpus; for chilli leaf spots see 14_chilli_other_diseases_conditions.

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, INTL) Early blight is caused by Alternaria solani [S1][S2].
- (OBS, INTL) It is mainly a disease of stressed or ageing plants and shows first on the oldest leaves [S6].

WEATHER FACTS
- (FAV, INTL, USA, POTATO-DERIVED) Spores form between about 5 and 30°C, best near 20°C. Alternating wet and dry periods favour spore production; few spores form on tissue that stays wet or dry all the time [S1].
- (OBS, INTL, USA, POTATO-DERIVED) Spore release peaks in mid-morning as dew dries, humidity falls and wind rises [S1].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) On tomato, disease develops at 15-27°C with an optimum of 28-30°C, and spreads most with wet weather, heavy dew or humidity of 90% or more [S2].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Long dew periods with night temperature above about 18°C, especially above 21°C, are most favourable. Long rain at warm temperature also favours it. Dry air or night temperature below about 13°C gives low risk [S3].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Another extension source gives 20-25°C with long periods of high humidity [S4]. Infection is fastest at 28-30°C when leaves are wet [S5].
- (WEAK, INTL) The sources give different temperature optima (about 20°C for spores in [S1]; 28-30°C in [S2] and [S5]; 20-25°C in [S4]). This corpus does not resolve the difference. Treat the 20-30°C band as a broad warm range.

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, INTL) Target-like spots with concentric rings and a yellow halo; also on stems and fruit [S4]. Stem lesions are dry, brown and ringed [S2].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, INTL) Inspect the oldest (lowest) leaves first [S6].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) There is no cure; prevention is key [S4].
- (OBS, INTL) Use drip rather than overhead irrigation, avoid working among wet plants, and removing up to one third of leaves early is described as acceptable [S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Start with disease-free seed. The source mentions hot-water seed treatment; its settings are not given here, so ask the KVK. Avoid low wet areas; resistant cultivars exist [S4].
- (OBS, INTL) Good fertilisation and irrigation reduce losses [S6].

PREVENTION
- (OBS, INTL) The fungus survives on crop debris, seed and nightshade-family weeds [S4][S5].
- (OBS, INTL) Spray-timing models using temperature and humidity exist [S3]. Product names, doses and timing are not included in this corpus; ask the KVK.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka or ICAR early blight threshold is present in this corpus.
- CORPUS RULE: Matching sensor readings show that conditions favour early blight. They do not confirm it. Field inspection is required.
