TITLE: Chilli viral diseases - mosaic, leaf curl, and other viruses
CROPS: chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: none reliable. The sources give no sensor threshold. Use only when the farmer reports viral symptoms.
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: Tomato leaf curl virus (whitefly vector) is in 12_tomato_leaf_curl_virus_whitefly.txt. This document covers chilli-specific viral diseases. Sensor readings cannot confirm viral diseases.

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, IN) Chilli mosaic (Cucumber mosaic virus - CMV): Spread by seed to some extent and by aphid vectors from collateral hosts [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Chilli leaf curl virus (ChiLCV): Carried by whitefly Bemisia tabaci. Primary spread by whiteflies, secondary by movement of infected plants [S2].
- (OBS, IN) Chilli veinal mottle virus (ChiVMV): Aphid-transmitted, causes mosaic and mottling [S3].
- (OBS, IN) Groundnut bud necrosis virus (GBNV) / Tomato spotted wilt virus (TSWV): Thrips-transmitted, causes bud necrosis and ringspots [S3].
- (WEAK, INTL) A research paper states that vector activity, virus multiplication and symptom development are affected by weather during and after infection, but gives no thresholds [S4].

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, IN) Chilli mosaic (CMV): Mosaic pattern of light and dark green on leaves; leaf distortion, curling, stunting; fruit may be small, distorted, with ringspots [S1][S3].
- (INSP, IN) Chilli leaf curl (ChiLCV): Severe stunting, downward rolling and crinkling of leaves, older leaves leathery and brittle, short internodes, bushy look with more side branches [S2].
- (INSP, IN) Whitefly feeding damage alone (no virus): Chlorotic spots, yellowing, and downward curling and drying of leaves [S5]. Field inspection needed to distinguish from viral leaf curl.
- (INSP, IN) Chilli veinal mottle (ChiVMV): Vein clearing, mottling, leaf distortion, stunting [S3].
- (INSP, IN) Bud necrosis (GBNV/TSWV): Necrosis of growing buds, concentric ringspots on leaves and fruit, stunting, plant death [S3].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, IN) Look for whiteflies on undersides of leaves (for leaf curl) [S2][S5].
- (INSP, IN) Look for aphids on growing tips (for mosaic, veinal mottle) [S1][S3].
- (INSP, IN) Look for thrips in flowers (for bud necrosis) [S3].
- (INSP, IN) Check for mosaic/mottle patterns on young leaves, leaf curling/stunting, and bushy appearance [S1][S2][S3].
- CORPUS NOTE: Whitefly feeding alone can curl leaves without virus [S5]. Aphid feeding can cause distortion. Only field inspection and KVK/agriculture officer confirmation (or lab test) can identify the virus. Sensor readings cannot suggest viral diseases.

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, IN) Uproot and destroy virus-infected plants [S1][S2].
- (OBS, IN) Use nitrogen and irrigation judiciously; lush growth attracts vectors [S2].
- (OBS, IN) Remove weed hosts: Abutilon indicum for whitefly/leaf curl; collateral hosts for aphids/thrips [S1][S2].
- (OBS, IN) Use yellow sticky traps for whitefly monitoring; blue sticky traps for thrips (trap numbers not given, ask KVK) [S2][S3].
- (OBS, IN) Use reflective mulches to repel aphids and whiteflies [S3].
- (OBS, IN) Seed treatment and insecticide sprays for vector management are mentioned; product names, doses and timing not included here. Ask KVK or agriculture officer for approved products [S1][S2][S3].

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No reliable sensor threshold exists in the retrieved sources for any chilli viral disease. Sensor readings must not be used to suggest them.
- CORPUS RULE: A virus cannot be confirmed from sensor readings. This document is only retrieved when the farmer reports viral symptoms.
