TITLE: Chilli soil moisture - field-relevant bands and crop-stage guidance
CROPS: chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct; temperature_c; rainfall_mm; irrigation events (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: This document summarizes field-relevant soil moisture information for chilli. It does NOT provide a universal soil_moisture_pct threshold. The sensor column soil_moisture_pct uses different units than research sources (field capacity %, % max water-holding capacity, kPa tension). Always confirm units before comparing.

CORPUS RULE: No universal soil moisture target exists. Ask the team for crop and soil-type targets, or a local KVK recommendation.

FIELD-RELEVANT CONTEXT FROM SOURCES

- (OBS, IN) NIPHM: chilli damping-off favoured by heavy rainfall, excessive/frequent irrigation, poorly drained soil, high soil moisture with 25-30°C (Pythium) or 30-35°C (Rhizoctonia) [S1].
- (OBS, IN) NIPHM: chilli Fusarium wilt favoured by relatively high soil moisture and soil temperature; spread by soil and implements [S1]. CORPUS NOTE: This differs from tomato Fusarium wilt (10) which is linked to LOW soil moisture. The difference is not resolved here.
- (OBS, IN) Phytophthora crown rot/wilt in chilli: waterlogged fields, high soil moisture, heavy rainfall, poor drainage, temperature 25-30°C [S2][S3].
- (OBS, INTL) Chilli root rot pathogens (Phytophthora, Pythium) thrive in wet, poorly drained soils [S2][S3][S4].
- (OBS, INTL) Rhizoctonia can cause disease in both wet and dry conditions, but is more common in warm, moist soil [S3][S4].
- (OBS, INTL) Blossom-end rot in chilli (physiological) is linked to fluctuating soil moisture and calcium transport issues, similar to tomato [S5].
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka chilli deficit irrigation or soil moisture band study was found in this corpus.

CROP STAGE GUIDANCE (general principles from Indian extension sources)

Establishment (transplant to 2 weeks):
- Keep soil consistently moist but well-drained; raised beds recommended [S1][S2].
- Avoid waterlogging which favours Phytophthora and Pythium [S2][S3].

Vegetative growth:
- Allow mild drying between irrigations to encourage deep rooting [S4].
- High soil moisture + warm temperature (30-35°C) → conditions favour Rhizoctonia [S1].

Flowering and fruit set:
- Maintain even soil moisture; avoid fluctuations [S5].
- High soil moisture + high humidity favours foliar diseases (anthracnose 13, bacterial leaf spot chilli_bacterial_leaf_spot, powdery mildew 06) [see relevant docs].

Fruit development to harvest:
- Continue even moisture; water stress reduces fruit size and pungency [S5].
- Avoid overhead irrigation near harvest to reduce anthracnose spread on ripening fruit [S6].

SENSOR READING INTERPRETATION HINTS

- Steadily HIGH soil_moisture_pct over days + temperature_c 25-30°C + heavy rainfall_mm → conditions favour Phytophthora/Pythium wilt and damping-off (chilli_wilt_damping_off).
- Steadily HIGH soil_moisture_pct + temperature_c 30-35°C → conditions favour Rhizoctonia (chilli_wilt_damping_off).
- HIGH soil_moisture_pct + warm soil temp (NIPHM) → conditions favour chilli Fusarium wilt per NIPHM (14_chilli_other) - note contradiction with tomato Fusarium.
- SWINGING soil_moisture_pct + high temperature_c + high soil_ec_dsm → conditions linked to blossom-end rot (physiological, similar to tomato 18).
- VERY HIGH soil_moisture_pct after heavy rainfall_mm + poor drainage reported → conditions favour Pythium/Phytophthora damping-off (chilli_wilt_damping_off, 11).
- CORPUS RULE: These are conditions that favour problems. They do not confirm any disease or disorder. Field inspection is required.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka-measured soil_moisture_pct bands for chilli in this corpus.
- (WEAK, IN) The NIPHM source gives temperature ranges with "high soil moisture" but does not define the soil moisture unit or value.
- CORPUS NOTE: The contradiction between chilli Fusarium wilt (high soil moisture per NIPHM) and tomato Fusarium wilt (low soil moisture per TNAU) is not resolved in this corpus.
