DOC_ID: 21_soil_moisture_effects_by_disease
TITLE: How soil moisture links to different problems (sourced statements side by side)
CROPS: tomato; chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct (high for days, low, or swinging)
EMBED: yes

HIGH OR WET SOIL
- (FAV, INTL, China) Bacterial wilt of tomato: disease outcome was positively linked to soil moisture in a field study [S1]. In a laboratory study, wilt was lower at 20-30% of maximum water-holding capacity [S2]. CORPUS NOTE: this is not the same unit as soil_moisture_pct. See 09_bacterial_wilt_ralstonia.
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Damping-off by Pythium and Phytophthora: cool, wet, poorly drained soil [S3]. Overwatering is linked to more damping-off [S4].
- (FAV, IN) Chilli damping-off: high soil moisture with 25-30°C (Pythium) or 30-35°C (Rhizoctonia) [S5]. Chilli Fusarium wilt: relatively high soil moisture and soil temperature [S5].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Gray mold on tomato was most severe in acidic sandy soil with high soil moisture in Florida [S6].
- (PHYS, INTL) Blossom-end rot (physiological): waterlogged soil, overwatering and root damage are listed as contributing factors [S7].

LOW OR DRY SOIL
- (FAV, IN) Fusarium wilt of tomato: TNAU lists low soil moisture, light sandy soil and low pH [S8].
- (PHYS, INTL) Blossom-end rot (physiological): Ontario states it is primarily caused by dry soil conditions, and swings between dry and wet are a trigger [S7][S9].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Damping-off by Rhizoctonia and Fusarium can occur in warm, dry conditions [S3][S10].
- (OBS, INTL) Early blight is mainly a disease of stressed or ageing plants [S11].

LEAF-LEVEL WETNESS (not soil)
- (FAV, INTL) Late blight needs long leaf wetness [S12]; powdery mildew does not need it and is harmed by free water [S13]. These concern the leaf surface, not soil_moisture_pct.

TARGET MOISTURE LEVELS
- UNAVAILABLE: The retrieved sources give no universal soil_moisture_pct target. Do not invent one. Ask the team for crop and soil-type targets, or a local KVK recommendation.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- CORPUS RULE: Wet-soil and dry-soil patterns overlap across diseases and disorders. Sensor soil_moisture_pct cannot confirm or separate them. Field inspection is required.
