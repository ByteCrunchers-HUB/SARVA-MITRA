TITLE: Nutrient deficiency vs toxicity discrimination - visual keys and sensor correlates
CROPS: tomato; chilli; ragi
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_ec_dsm; soil_moisture_pct; temperature_c; sunlight_wm2
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: This document supplements tomato_nutrient_deficiencies.txt and chilli_nutrient_deficiencies.txt with discrimination keys. Sensor readings indicate conditions that favour nutrient disorders, but cannot confirm specific deficiencies. Soil test is the only reliable confirmation. KVK soil testing lab recommended.

CORPUS RULE: Nutrient disorders are physiological, not infectious. Sensors show conditions that favour them. They do not confirm any deficiency or toxicity. Soil test and KVK confirmation required.

IDENTIFICATION
- (PHYS, IN) Nutrient imbalances cause characteristic visual symptoms, but several deficiencies look similar (e.g., N vs S, Mg vs Fe, K vs drought) [S1][S2].
- (PHYS, IN) Toxicity symptoms (excess) can mimic deficiency of other nutrients [S1].
- (PHYS, IN) Soil pH, EC, moisture, and temperature affect nutrient availability - not just soil content [S1][S2].

DISCRIMINATION KEYS BY NUTRIENT

NITROGEN (N) vs SULPHUR (S) - Both cause yellowing:
- N deficiency: OLDER leaves first, UNIFORM yellowing from tips, stunted growth [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- S deficiency: YOUNG leaves first, uniform yellowing (S immobile), stunted [S1].
- Discrimination: Leaf age affected. N = old leaves; S = young leaves [S1].
- Sensor: LOW soil_moisture_pct reduces N mineralization; HIGH soil_ec_dsm may indicate S excess [S2].

PHOSPHORUS (P) - Distinctive:
- P deficiency: OLDER leaves, dark green with PURPLISH undersides, stunted [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- Few conditions mimic this; cold soil induces temporary P deficiency [S1].
- Sensor: LOW temperature_c (<15°C) + LOW soil_moisture_pct = reduced P uptake [S2].

POTASSIUM (K) vs DROUGHT vs SALT STRESS - All cause edge burn:
- K deficiency: OLDER leaves, MARGINAL NECROSIS (edge burn), curling, interveinal chlorosis [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- Drought stress: ALL leaves wilt, recover at night, edge burn from tip [S2].
- Salt stress (HIGH soil_ec_dsm): Edge burn + leaf thickening + general stunting [20_soil_salinity_ec_tomato, S4].
- Discrimination: K = older leaves only, curling; Drought = all leaves, diurnal wilting; Salt = all leaves, high soil_ec_dsm [S1][S2].
- Sensor: HIGH soil_ec_dsm (>2.5 dS/m tomato) = salt; LOW soil_moisture_pct = drought; normal soil_moisture_pct + normal EC = K [S2].

CALCIUM (Ca) vs BORON (B) - Both affect growing points:
- Ca deficiency: YOUNG leaves, distorted/hooked, tip burn, blossom-end rot (fruit) [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- B deficiency: YOUNG leaves, brittle/thickened, growing point dies, "rosette", cracked fruit [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- Discrimination: Ca = BER on fruit + tip burn; B = growing point death + rosette + cracked fruit [S1].
- Sensor: SWINGING soil_moisture_pct = Ca/BER trigger; HIGH soil_ec_dsm reduces both Ca and B uptake [S2].

MAGNESIUM (Mg) vs IRON (Fe) vs MANGANESE (Mn) - Interveinal chlorosis:
- Mg deficiency: OLDER leaves, interveinal chlorosis (veins GREEN), necrotic spots later [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- Fe deficiency: YOUNG leaves, interveinal chlorosis (veins GREEN), severe = white leaves [S1].
- Mn deficiency: YOUNG leaves, interveinal chlorosis with brown necrotic spots [S1].
- Discrimination: Mg = OLD leaves; Fe/Mn = YOUNG leaves. Mn has necrotic spots; Fe does not [S1].
- Sensor: HIGH soil_ec_dsm reduces all; LOW temperature_c reduces Fe/Mn uptake [S2].

ZINC (Zn) - Critical for all three crops:
- Zn deficiency: YOUNG leaves, SMALL, distorted, "khaira disease" appearance, short internodes, rosette [S1][S3].
- Ragi: Zn deficiency common in Karnataka alkaline soils; "khaira disease" in nursery [S3].
- Sensor: HIGH soil_ec_dsm + HIGH pH (alkaline) = Zn deficiency risk [S2].
- UNAVAILABLE: Karnataka soil_moisture_pct thresholds for Zn availability.

TOXICITY SYMPTOMS (EXCESS)
- N excess: Excessive vegetative growth, dark green, delayed flowering, lodging, disease susceptibility [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1][chilli_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
- P excess: Rare; induces Zn/Fe deficiency [S1].
- K excess: Induces Mg/Ca deficiency (cation competition) [S1].
- B toxicity: OLDER leaf tips/edges necrotic, yellowing [S1].
- Salt toxicity (HIGH soil_ec_dsm): See 20_soil_salinity_ec_tomato [S4].

SENSOR CONDITIONS FAVOURING NUTRIENT DISORDERS

General:
- HIGH soil_ec_dsm (>2.5 dS/m tomato; chilli/ragi UNAVAILABLE): Reduces uptake of most nutrients [S2][20].
- LOW soil_moisture_pct sustained: Reduces mass flow of N, Ca, Mg, S [S2].
- SWINGING soil_moisture_pct: Disrupts Ca transport (BER) [18].
- LOW temperature_c (<15°C): Reduces P, Fe, Mn, Zn uptake [S2].
- HIGH temperature_c (>35°C): Reduces nutrient uptake, increases demand [S2].
- HIGH sunlight_wm2: Increases demand, can induce deficiencies in fast growth [S2].

Crop-Specific (Karnataka):
- Ragi in alkaline soils (North Karnataka): Zn, Fe deficiency common [S3].
- Tomato/chilli in high EC soils: Ca, Mg, K deficiencies common [S2].
- Tomato/chilli in sandy soils (red loam): N, K, S leaching common [S1].

FIELD INSPECTION - DISCRIMINATION CHECKLIST
1. Which leaves affected? (Old vs Young) → N/P/K/Mg = Old; Ca/B/Fe/Mn/Zn/S = Young
2. Pattern? (Uniform vs Interveinal vs Marginal) → N/S = Uniform; Mg/Fe/Mn = Interveinal; K/B/Salt = Marginal
3. Fruit symptoms? → Ca = BER; B = cracked; K = blotchy ripening
4. Soil test? → ESSENTIAL for confirmation [S1][S2].
5. Sensor context? → Check soil_ec_dsm, soil_moisture_pct, temperature_c trends [S2].

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES (NO DOSES)
- (OBS, IN) Soil test before every season; fertilise based on recommendation [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Split N application: basal + top-dress at critical stages [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Organic manures improve soil health, buffer pH/EC [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Foliar sprays for micronutrients (Zn, Fe, B) when soil application ineffective [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Lime acidic soils; gypsum for sodic soils; consult KVK [S1].
- (OBS, IN) Avoid excess N - increases disease/pest susceptibility [tomato_nutrient_deficiencies, S1].
