DOC_ID: 07_gray_mold_botrytis_tomato
TITLE: Gray mold / Botrytis blight (Botrytis cinerea) - conditions that favour it
CROPS: tomato; chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: humidity_pct; temperature_c; leaf_wet (night); soil_moisture_pct; canopy density (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, INTL) Gray mold is caused by Botrytis cinerea. On tomato it enters through wounds, dead tissue and fading flowers [S2].

WEATHER FACTS
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Disease occurs at 15.5-24°C, with an optimum of 15.5-21°C, and humidity above 80%. Temperature above about 28°C suppresses it [S1].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) It thrives at humidity above 90% and 18-24°C [S2].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) It is favoured at 18-24°C and needs only high humidity, not leaf wetness [S3].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Infection does not need a long humid spell; humid canopy air at night is often enough. Dense planting raises risk [S4].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Spores germinate when free water is present from rain, dew, fog or irrigation [S5].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) In Florida it was most severe on plants in acidic, sandy soil with high soil moisture [S6].
- (FAV, IN) For chilli, TNAU notes that a dense canopy creates long leaf wetness at night and raises severity, and velvety growth appears under cool humid conditions [S7].
- (WEAK, INTL) Sources differ on whether leaf wetness is needed ([S3] says not; [S5] says free water helps germination). This corpus does not resolve it.

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, INTL) Light tan or grey spots on leaves that become covered with grey-brown fuzzy growth; stem lesions that can girdle the stem; soft rot and "ghost spots" on fruit [S4][S8].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, INTL) Check wounds, dead tissue and fading flowers, and dense parts of the canopy, for grey-brown fuzzy growth [S2][S4].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Keep humidity and leaf wetness low: ventilation, avoid overhead irrigation, roll up tunnel sides where tunnels are used [S1]. In greenhouses keep humidity below 90% [S8].
- (OBS, IN) For chilli, remove and burn decaying infected plant parts [S7].
- (OBS, INTL) The source states no tomato varieties are resistant (USA context) [S1]. Fungicides are used only after cultural steps; product names, doses and timing are not included here. Ask the KVK.
- (WEAK, INTL) One source links a calcium-to-phosphorus ratio below 2:1 with higher susceptibility [S3]. Evidence is from greenhouse and tunnel advice only.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka threshold for gray mold is present in this corpus. Most sources describe greenhouse, tunnel or cool-climate tomato.
- CORPUS RULE: Matching sensor readings show that conditions favour gray mold. They do not confirm it.
