TITLE: Tomato soil moisture - field-relevant bands and crop-stage guidance
CROPS: tomato
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct; temperature_c; rainfall_mm; irrigation events (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

CORPUS NOTE: This document summarizes field-relevant soil moisture information for tomato. It does NOT provide a universal soil_moisture_pct threshold. The sensor column soil_moisture_pct is a volumetric or gravimetric percentage; sources use different units (field capacity %, % of max water-holding capacity, kPa tension). Always confirm units before comparing.

CORPUS RULE: No universal soil moisture target exists. Ask the team for crop and soil-type targets, or a local KVK recommendation.

FIELD-RELEVANT CONTEXT FROM SOURCES

- (OBS, INTL) Tomato is moderately sensitive to water stress. Both water deficit and excess reduce yield and quality [S1][S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Blossom-end rot (BER, physiological) is linked to FLUCTUATING soil moisture - swings between dry and wet. Ontario states it is primarily caused by dry soil conditions [S3][S4].
- (OBS, INTL) Bacterial wilt (09) is favoured by HIGH soil moisture with high temperature (29-35°C) [S5].
- (OBS, IN) Fusarium wilt (10) is linked to LOW soil moisture, light sandy soil, low pH [S6].
- (OBS, INTL) Damping-off by Pythium/Phytophthora: cool, wet, poorly drained soil [S7]. Rhizoctonia/Fusarium damping-off: warm, dry conditions [S7].
- (OBS, INTL) Gray mold (07) was most severe in acidic sandy soil with high soil moisture in Florida [S8].
- (OBS, INTL, USA) Deficit irrigation study (California): sustained deficit at 50% ETc reduced yield but improved fruit quality; mild deficit (75-85% ETc) maintained yield [S1].
- (OBS, IN, Karnataka) UHS Bagalkote study (Northern Dry Zone): four irrigation levels at 100%, 80%, 60%, 40% of cumulative pan evaporation (CPE). Highest tomato yield and water-use efficiency at 80% CPE + polythene mulch [S9][S10].
- (OBS, IN, Karnataka) Same study: soil moisture at 80% CPE with mulch maintained better soil moisture and temperature regime [S9].

CROP STAGE GUIDANCE (general principles from extension sources)

Establishment (transplant to 2 weeks):
- Keep soil consistently moist but not waterlogged to promote root establishment [S2][S4].
- Avoid dry-down that stresses young plants.

Vegetative growth (2-6 weeks):
- Allow mild drying between irrigations to encourage deep rooting [S1][S2].
- Soil should not remain saturated; high soil moisture + high temp favours bacterial wilt [S5].

Flowering and fruit set:
- CRITICAL: Avoid moisture stress. Fluctuations trigger blossom-end rot [S3][S4].
- Maintain even soil moisture; do not let soil dry out [S4].
- High humidity + high soil moisture favours foliar diseases (late blight, early blight, gray mold) [see 02, 03, 04, 07].

Fruit development to harvest:
- Continue even moisture; severe deficit reduces fruit size and quality [S1].
- Mild deficit near harvest can improve fruit sugar (Brix) but risks BER if fluctuations occur [S1][S3].

SENSOR READING INTERPRETATION HINTS

- Steadily HIGH soil_moisture_pct over days + high temperature_c (29-35°C) → conditions favour bacterial wilt (09) and damping-off (11).
- Steadily LOW soil_moisture_pct + light sandy soil reported by farmer → conditions favour Fusarium wilt (10).
- SWINGING soil_moisture_pct (dry-wet-dry cycles) + high temperature_c + high soil_ec_dsm → conditions linked to blossom-end rot (18).
- VERY HIGH soil_moisture_pct after heavy rainfall_mm + poor drainage reported → conditions favour Pythium/Phytophthora damping-off (11).
- CORPUS RULE: These are conditions that favour problems. They do not confirm any disease or disorder. Field inspection is required.

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka-measured soil_moisture_pct bands (e.g., "60-70%") for tomato in this corpus. The UHS Bagalkote study used % of cumulative pan evaporation, not soil_moisture_pct sensor units.
- CORPUS NOTE: The 80% CPE + mulch result is the most Karnataka-relevant finding but uses different units than the sensor. It cannot be directly compared to soil_moisture_pct readings.
- All other values are from international or non-Karnataka Indian sources.
