TITLE: Quick lookup of sourced weather and soil conditions that favour each disease or disorder
CROPS: tomato; chilli; ragi (finger millet)
SENSOR_SIGNALS: humidity_pct; leaf_wet; temperature_c; soil_moisture_pct; rainfall_mm; sunlight_wm2; soil_ec_dsm

PURPOSE
- CORPUS RULE: This is a lookup index. Each line repeats a statement from a disease file named in brackets, with its own source tag [S#] listed in this document's REFERENCES. Use the disease file for detail. Every value shows conditions that favour a problem. No value here confirms a disease.

ORIGIN SUMMARY
- CORPUS NOTE: Karnataka/UAS Bengaluru values are present only for ragi (see 16_ragi_weather_uas_bengaluru_tables, uncertain alignment). India values (TNAU, NIPHM, ICAR) cover chilli and some tomato and ragi items; TNAU is a Tamil Nadu body. All other values are international research from the USA, Canada, Australia, France and elsewhere. Ragi blast temperature and cloud conditions come from course (secondary) material.

LEAF WETNESS AND HUMIDITY
- Late blight, tomato (03): 10-12 hours of leaf wetness at 15-21°C favours spore formation [S3]. Cool 10-21°C with humidity above 80% and dew or fog [S1]. A potato/tomato risk model counts 10 hours of leaf wetness or humidity above 90% at temperature above 10°C [S2]. (FAV, INTL)
- Early blight, tomato (04): alternating wet and dry periods; spores form between about 5 and 30°C [S4]. Disease develops at 15-27°C with humidity of 90% or more or heavy dew [S5]. (FAV, INTL)
- Septoria leaf spot, tomato (05): high humidity, 20-25°C, dew or wet leaves, poor airflow [S6]. (FAV, INTL)
- Powdery mildew, tomato and chilli (06): high humidity with about 16-25°C; free water on leaves is not needed and harms spores [S7]. For chilli, cool dry weather favours spore germination and high humidity favours development [S8]. (FAV, INTL and IN)
- Gray mold, tomato and chilli (07): humidity above 80% at 15.5-24°C [S9]; needs high humidity, not leaf wetness [S10]. For chilli, a dense canopy gives long night leaf wetness [S11]. (FAV, INTL and IN)
- Bacterial leaf spot, tomato and chilli (08): moist weather, splashing rain, high humidity or persistent dew; no temperature threshold available [S12]. (FAV, IN)
- Chilli anthracnose (13): about 28°C with humidity of 95% [S13]; humidity above 97% in another Indian source [S14]. (FAV, IN)
- Ragi blast (15): humidity above 90%, cloudy skies, frequent rain or drizzle, 25-28°C for spore germination [S15]. Secondary course material. (FAV, SEC)
- Ragi blast, UAS Bengaluru tables (16): morning humidity 90-94% and more than 3 cloudy days in the Zone 6 (Mysore) tables [S16]. Alignment is uncertain. (FAV, KA, WEAK)

SOIL CONDITIONS
- Bacterial wilt (09): high soil moisture with about 29-35°C [S17]. (FAV, INTL)
- Fusarium wilt of tomato (10): about 28°C, low soil moisture, light sandy acidic soil [S18]. (FAV, IN)
- Damping-off (11): Pythium and Phytophthora in cool, wet, poorly drained soil; Fusarium and Rhizoctonia more common in warm, dry conditions [S19]. For chilli, high soil moisture with 25-30°C (Pythium) or 30-35°C (Rhizoctonia) [S14]. (FAV, INTL and IN)
- Blossom-end rot, tomato (18, physiological disorder): dry or fluctuating soil moisture, heat and high salinity are listed factors [S20]. Ontario states it is primarily caused by dry soil [S21]. (PHYS, INTL)
- Salt stress, tomato (20, physiological): the yield threshold is 2.5 dS/m (ECe) [S22]. (PHYS, INTL)

TEMPERATURE EXTREMES
- Heat stress, tomato (19, physiological): day/night temperature above 26/20°C can significantly affect fruit set and yield [S23]. (PHYS, INTL)

SUNLIGHT
- Late blight spores survive about 1 hour in sunny dry conditions and up to about 4 hours in cloudy weather [S3]. (FAV, INTL)
- Powdery mildew spores are sensitive to extreme heat (above about 32°C) and direct sunlight [S24]. (FAV, INTL)

NOT AVAILABLE
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka-measured thresholds are available for tomato or chilli diseases in this corpus.
- UNAVAILABLE: Thresholds for chilli heat stress, chilli salinity and ragi salinity are not available.
- UNAVAILABLE: No universal soil_moisture_pct target is given by any source (see 21_soil_moisture_effects_by_disease).
