TITLE: Late blight (Phytophthora infestans) - weather conditions that favour it
CROPS: tomato
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature_c; humidity_pct; leaf_wet; rainfall_mm; sunlight_wm2 (cloud indicator); irrigation timing (farmer-reported)
EMBED: yes

IDENTIFICATION
- (OBS, INTL) Late blight on tomato and potato is caused by Phytophthora infestans [S1].
- (OBS, INTL) It can affect leaves, stems, branches and fruit (and tubers on potato) [S4].

WEATHER FACTS
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Cool temperature of 10-21°C with humidity above 80% from heavy dew, fog or rain, plus long leaf wetness, favours the disease [S1].
- (FAV, INTL, Canada, POTATO-DERIVED) Risk is highest with frequent rain, heavy dew or fog, long leaf wetness, high humidity, and overhead or night irrigation [S2].
- (FAV, INTL, USA, POTATO-DERIVED) A research risk model counts "spores forming" after 10 consecutive hours of leaf wetness, or humidity above 90%, when temperature is above 10°C [S3].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Spore production is favoured at 18-21°C with humidity near 100%. Spores last far less when humidity is below 95%; at 80% humidity they survive only about 5 hours [S4].
- (FAV, INTL, USA) Leaf wetness of more than 10-12 hours at 15-21°C favours spore formation and spread. Spores survive up to about 1 hour in sunny dry conditions and up to about 4 hours in cloudy weather [S5].
- (FAV, INTL, USA, POTATO-DERIVED) A Michigan study states favourable conditions as air temperature of 7-27°C with 10 or more hours of leaf wetness [S6].
- (OBS, INTL) Hot, dry conditions turn lesions brown and papery [S1].

SYMPTOMS
- (INSP, INTL) Water-soaked dark green patches on leaves; under humidity above 80% they turn purple to black with white spore growth [S1].
- (INSP, INTL) Lesions are not limited by leaf veins, may look greasy, and can destroy a leaf in days. White mildew-like growth shows on the underside of leaves on humid mornings. Badly affected plants may smell [S2].

FIELD INSPECTION
- (INSP, INTL) Scout low-lying, shaded and hard-to-spray parts of the field more often [S2][S7].
- (INSP, INTL) Check the underside of leaves on humid mornings for white mildew-like growth [S2].

MANAGEMENT
- (OBS, INTL) Management is preventive. The source states there is no economic threshold [S2].
- (OBS, INTL) Weather-based forecasting tools are used to time control measures [S2][S3].
- (OBS, INTL) Sources describe protectant fungicides as preventive tools used before infection [S1][S4]. Product names, doses and timing are deliberately not included in this corpus. Ask the KVK or agriculture officer for approved products.

PREVENTION
- (OBS, INTL) Avoid overhead and night-time irrigation, since they extend leaf wetness [S2].

EVIDENCE QUALITY AND LIMITS
- UNAVAILABLE: No Karnataka or ICAR late blight weather threshold is present in this corpus. All thresholds above are from North American studies, several on potato.
- CORPUS RULE: Matching sensor readings show that conditions favour late blight. They do not confirm it. Only field inspection of the plant, with KVK or agriculture officer confirmation, can identify the disease.
