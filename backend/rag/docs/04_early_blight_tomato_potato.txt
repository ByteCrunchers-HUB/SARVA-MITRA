DOC_ID: 04_early_blight_tomato_potato
TITLE: Early blight (Alternaria solani) - warm weather with wet/dry cycles
CROPS: tomato, potato, brinjal, pepper/chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature about 20-30 C; warm nights; long dew or humidity 90%+; leaf_wet alternating with dry spells; stressed or older plants

WEATHER FACTS
- Spores form between about 5 and 30 C (41-86 F), best near 20 C (68 F). Alternating wet and dry periods favour spore production; few spores form on tissue that stays wet or dry all the time [S1].
- Spore release peaks in mid-morning as dew dries, humidity falls and wind rises [S1].
- Tomato disease develops at 15-27 C (59-80 F) with an optimum of 28-30 C (82-86 F); it spreads most with wet weather, heavy dew or humidity of 90% or more [S2].
- Long dew periods with night temperatures above about 18 C (65 F), especially above 21 C (70 F), are most favourable. Long rain at warm temperatures also favours it. Dry air or night temperatures below about 13 C (55 F) give low risk [S3].
- Another extension source gives 20-25 C (68-77 F) with long periods of high humidity [S4]. Infection is fastest at 28-30 C when wet [S5].
- Early blight is mainly a disease of stressed or ageing plants and shows first on the oldest leaves [S6].

SYMPTOMS
- Target-like spots with concentric rings and a yellow halo; also on stems and fruit [S4]. Lesions on stems are dry brown with rings [S2].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- There is no cure; prevention is key [S4].
- Use drip rather than overhead irrigation; avoid working in wet plants; removing up to one third of leaves early is acceptable [S2].
- Start with disease-free seed; hot water seed treatment is mentioned (50 C for 25 minutes); avoid low wet areas; resistant cultivars exist [S4].
- Good fertilisation and irrigation reduce losses [S6]. The fungus survives on crop debris, seed and nightshade-family weeds [S4][S5].
- Spray timing models using temperature and humidity exist [S3]. Follow label and local advice.
