DOC_ID: 02_weather_thresholds_by_disease
TITLE: Quick lookup of weather and soil conditions favouring each disease (sourced table)
TYPE: reference index. Each line repeats a statement from the disease file named in brackets. Use the disease files for detail.

LEAF WETNESS / HUMIDITY
- Late blight: 10 or more hours of leaf wetness, or humidity above 90%, with temperature above 10 C starts spore formation [Oregon State model, file 03]. Humidity above 80% with cool 10-21 C weather and dew/fog [S UF/IFAS, file 03].
- Early blight: alternating wet and dry periods, temperatures roughly 20-30 C; few spores form on tissue that is always wet or always dry [file 04].
- Septoria leaf spot: high humidity, 20-25 C optimum, dew/wet leaves, poor airflow [file 05].
- Powdery mildew: moderate temperature and high humidity; does NOT need wet leaves; free water harms spores [file 06].
- Gray mold (Botrytis): humidity above 80-90%, 15-24 C; needs humidity, not leaf wetness [file 07].
- Bacterial leaf spot: moist weather, splashing rain, high humidity or persistent dew [file 08].
- Chilli anthracnose: about 28 C with humidity 95% or more [file 13].
- Ragi blast: humidity above 90%, cloudy days, drizzle, 25-28 C [file 15].

SOIL CONDITIONS
- Bacterial wilt: high soil moisture and high temperature (about 29-35 C) [file 09].
- Fusarium wilt of tomato: soil temperature about 28 C; dry/low soil moisture, light sandy acidic soil [file 10].
- Damping-off: Pythium and Phytophthora in cool, wet soil; Fusarium and Rhizoctonia can occur in warm, dry soil [file 11].
- Blossom-end rot: uneven soil moisture, heat, high salinity [file 18].
- Salt stress in tomato: yield declines above roughly 2.5 dS/m [file 20].

TEMPERATURE BANDS (approximate)
Cool 10-21 C: late blight. Moderate 15-27 C: powdery mildew, gray mold, Septoria. Warm 20-30 C: early blight, anthracnose, ragi blast. Hot 29-35 C: bacterial wilt; heat stress on tomato flowering above about 26/20 C day/night [file 19].

SUNLIGHT
- Late blight spores live about an hour in sunny dry weather and up to about four hours in cloudy weather [file 03].
- Powdery mildew spores and fungus are sensitive to extreme heat and direct sun [file 06].
