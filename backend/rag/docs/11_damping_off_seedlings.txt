TITLE: Damping-off of seedlings and nursery plants
CROPS: all vegetable seedlings (tomato, chilli, brinjal); millet seedlings (ragi)
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct very high; cool wet soil; low light/cloudy days; poor drainage; overwatering

FACTS
- Caused by several soil organisms: Pythium, Phytophthora, Rhizoctonia and Fusarium [S1][S2].
- Pythium and Phytophthora favour cool, wet, poorly drained soil; Fusarium and Rhizoctonia are more common under warm, dry conditions. Cool wet soil slows seedling growth, which lengthens the susceptible period [S3]. UMass also notes Rhizoctonia can cause disease in dry soil [S4].
- Low light, overwatering, high salts from over-fertilising and cool soil are linked to more damping-off [S5].
- If cloudy, cool weather keeps trays wet for days, Pythium is likely; Pythium also shows in low spots where plants sit in water [S2]. Queensland notes Pythium and Rhizoctonia are common in both tropical and temperate soils and thrive in wet soil, especially when temperature suits the pathogen more than the host [S6].
- For chilli, NIPHM lists heavy rainfall, excessive and frequent irrigation, poorly drained soil, close spacing and high soil moisture with about 25-30 C for Pythium, and about 30-35 C for Rhizoctonia [S7].
- Seedlings that are killed cannot be saved; prevention is the only way [S8]. Established plants resist better [S9].

SYMPTOMS
- Seeds rot or seedlings fail to emerge; stems at or below the soil line become water-soaked and collapse; seedlings fall over [S5][S8].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Do not overwater; water in late morning when soil is dry; use well-drained soil or raised beds; do not plant into cool soil; do not sow too densely or too deep [S3][S4][S8].
- Use clean, sterilised trays and clean potting mix; treated seed can reduce incidence [S5][S10].
