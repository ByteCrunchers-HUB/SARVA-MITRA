DOC_ID: 05_septoria_leaf_spot_tomato
TITLE: Septoria leaf spot (Septoria lycopersici)
CROPS: tomato and other nightshade-family plants
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature about 15-27 C (best 20-25 C); high humidity; leaf wetness or long dew; heat with humidity; poor airflow

WEATHER FACTS
- Favourable: high humidity, moderate temperature 20-25 C (68-77 F), high dew point or wet conditions, poor airflow [S1].
- Long high humidity with 15-27 C (60-80 F) and leaf wetness are ideal [S2].
- Heat waves with long stretches of high temperature and humidity, or repeated periods of wet foliage such as long morning dew, favour it [S3].
- A growth-chamber study found most lesions at 20-25 C, though lesions still formed at 10 C. With humidity of 50-60% and no dew, lesion numbers were similar to 16 hours of dew on inoculated leaves, so low levels can occur even under unfavourable conditions [S4].
- Spreads by splashing rain, equipment, workers or insects; survives on tomato debris, solanaceous weeds and can be seed-borne [S3].

SYMPTOMS
- Many small circular spots on the lowest, oldest leaves, with dark margins and tan to grey centres with tiny black dots; spreads upward; leaves yellow, brown and wither [S2]. Fruit infection is rare [S2]; it does not directly affect fruit [S5].
- It can be harder to control than early blight [S3].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Remove lower growth, keep spacing, control host weeds, destroy debris, avoid over-irrigating [S1]. Water at the base; prune the lowest 3-4 leaf branches once plants are established [S6].
- If caught early remove infected lower leaves; removing leaves above fruit weakens the plant and exposes fruit to sunscald [S2].
- Preventative sprays with copper or chlorothalonil are named; follow label [S1].
