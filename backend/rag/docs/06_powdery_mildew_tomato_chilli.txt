DOC_ID: 06_powdery_mildew_tomato_chilli
TITLE: Powdery mildew of tomato and chilli (Leveillula taurica and Oidium species)
CROPS: tomato, chilli, capsicum, brinjal, many vegetables
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature about 15-30 C; humidity moderate to high; leaf_wet = 0; rainfall_mm = 0 for days; shade or low light; dense canopy

WEATHER FACTS
- It does not need standing water on leaves; high humidity with moderate temperatures (about 16-25 C, 60-77 F) favours it, and hot temperatures with low humidity limit it [S1].
- It thrives in warm, dry weather with moderate to high humidity and can be suppressed by rain; optimum 15-27 C (60-81 F), infection below 30 C [S2].
- Spores are killed and germination is inhibited by free moisture; moderate temperatures and shade favour it; spores are sensitive to extreme heat (above about 32 C / 90 F) and direct sunlight [S3][S4].
- Germination and infection occur at temperatures under 30 C and at medium to high humidity; common in warm, dry weather [S5].
- In greenhouses, optimum is 20-30 C (68-86 F), humidity above 95% and fairly low light [S6].
- For chilli (TNAU/NIPHM): cool dry weather favours spore germination and high relative humidity favours disease development [S7]. A TNAU weather advisory notes high humidity leading to powdery mildew in chilli [S8].

SYMPTOMS
- Pale yellow blotches on the upper leaf surface; white powdery growth mostly on the lower surface (chilli/tomato Leveillula) [S5][S7]. Leaves may yellow, die, drop; fruit can get sunscald [S4][S5]. Seldom kills plants but can sharply cut yield [S1].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Avoid too much nitrogen (lush growth favours the disease); remove volunteer plants; use resistant varieties where available [S5].
- Space plants about 24 inches or more for air flow; avoid shady low fields [S2].
- Named options: sulphur (can burn plants in hot humid conditions), potassium silicate (preventive only), some biofungicides [S1]. TNAU names wettable sulphur or carbendazim for chilli [S7]. Follow label and local advice.
