DOC_ID: 09_bacterial_wilt_ralstonia
TITLE: Bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum) - hot, wet soil
CROPS: tomato, brinjal, chilli/pepper, potato, ginger and other hosts
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_moisture_pct high for days; temperature about 29-35 C; recent heavy rain or irrigation

FACTS
- It is one of the most devastating tomato diseases in tropical and subtropical regions; the bacterium lives in soil and has a very wide host range [S1].
- Favoured by high temperature (about 29-35 C, 85-95 F) and high soil moisture. Infested soil and surface or irrigation water are the main sources; the bacterium enters roots, usually through wounds [S2].
- In a lab study, wilt was lower in drier soil (20-30% of maximum water-holding capacity) and the pathogen survived less in dry soil [S3]. A field study found disease outcome was positively linked to soil moisture, and suggested managing soil moisture could reduce losses [S4].
- Warm months increase incidence; in a China field study shifting transplanting time to avoid the hottest period reduced wilt [S1][S5]. Higher temperature also raised wilt severity in some resistant tomatoes [S5][S6]. Cold-water irrigation reduced wilt in a pot study [S7].
- Chemical control is often ineffective because the bacteria sit deep in soil [S6]. The UF/IFAS sheet states no known resistance in commercial cultivars (US context) [S2]; research on temperature-stable resistance genes is ongoing [S6].

SYMPTOMS
- Rapid and complete wilting of normal-looking grown plants [S8].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Pathogen-free soil, irrigation water, transplants and tools are important [S2].
- Managing soil moisture and planting date may reduce loss [S4][S5].
