TITLE: Soil salinity (electrical conductivity) and tomato yield
CROPS: tomato (moderately sensitive to salt); the threshold idea applies to other crops with different numbers
SENSOR_SIGNALS: soil_ec_dsm above about 2.5; rising trend; also high-EC irrigation water

FACTS
- Every crop tolerates some salt in the root zone; the highest level with no yield loss is the "salinity threshold", and above it yield falls in proportion to salt level. Soil salinity is expressed as the EC of a saturated soil extract (ECe) in dS/m (same as mmhos/cm) [S1][S2].
- Tomato threshold is 2.5 dS/m, with about 9.9% yield loss per dS/m above it (classic Maas-Hoffman values) [S2][S3]. Other studies report thresholds of about 2.97 and 3.31 dS/m with slopes of 11-15% per dS/m [S3].
- Tomato is moderately sensitive; growth and yield begin to decline when nutrient-solution EC exceeds about 2.5-4.0 dS/m. Moderate salinity can improve fruit sugar quality while lowering yield [S4].
- Irrigation water up to 2.5 dS/m did not have major harmful effects on marketable yield in one study; potassium fertiliser helped reduce the harm and blossom-end rot [S3].
- In drip irrigation, water of 4.5 dS/m cut yield about 10-12% compared with 1.2 dS/m [S5]. In a three-year study, keeping irrigation water salinity under 4 dS/m and soil matric potential above -20 kPa helped maintain yield; BER rose at 3-4 dS/m, and 5.5 dS/m cut yield by roughly 12-31% [S6].
- Drip irrigation with careful management was recommended when saline water must be used, since fruit yield per unit of water was higher [S7].
- Salinity also reduces leaf area and chlorophyll at high levels [S6]. Salinity links to blossom-end rot (file 18).
- COMPILER NOTE (not from a source): lab values are ECe on a saturated paste. A field sensor reading depends on soil moisture and sensor type, so treat it as a trend indicator and confirm with a lab test.
