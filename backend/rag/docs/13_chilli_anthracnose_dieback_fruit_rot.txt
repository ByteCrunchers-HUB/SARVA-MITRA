TITLE: Chilli anthracnose, dieback and fruit rot (Colletotrichum)
CROPS: chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature about 28-30 C; humidity 95-100% (morning humidity 84-97%); rainfall at frequent intervals, especially when fruits start to ripen

FACTS
- TNAU: favourable temperature 28 C and humidity 95%; high humidity when rain occurs after fruits start to ripen [S1].
- NIPHM: temperature 28 C with humidity above 97%, humid weather with frequent rainfall, and intercropping with turmeric (another host) [S2].
- A Haryana (CCSHAU) study found disease development was highest at 30 C and 100% humidity in the lab. In the field, maximum temperature 30.6-35.9 C, minimum 21.7-26.5 C, morning humidity 84.3-97% and evening humidity 54.3-58.3% suited the disease; minimum and maximum temperature together explained 85% of severity [S3].
- Spread: seed-borne; airborne spores and rain; flies and other insects carry spores between fruits [S1].
- Symptoms: black circular spots on ripening fruit; badly affected fruit turn straw or white and lose pungency; brownish-black spots on leaves; dieback of branches [S1][S2].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Use disease-free seed; seed treatment (thiram or zineb named); three sprays of captan at set stages (before flowering, fruit formation, and a fortnight later) per TNAU; biocontrol agents such as Pseudomonas fluorescens and Bacillus subtilis were effective in studies [S1].
- A TNAU advisory suggests mancozeb 2-3 times at 15-day intervals to prevent dieback and fruit rot [S4]. Doses must come from the label and local KVK.
