DOC_ID: 07_gray_mold_botrytis_tomato
TITLE: Gray mold / Botrytis blight (Botrytis cinerea)
CROPS: tomato (mostly greenhouse/tunnel and cool humid periods), chilli
SENSOR_SIGNALS: humidity above 80-90%; temperature about 15-24 C; humid nights; dense canopy; wounded or ageing plants

WEATHER FACTS
- Disease occurs at 15.5-24 C (60-75 F), optimum 15.5-21 C (60-70 F), with humidity above 80%; temperatures above about 28 C (82 F) suppress it [S1].
- It thrives at humidity above 90% and 18-24 C (65-75 F) and enters through wounds, dead tissue and fading flowers [S2].
- It is favoured at 18-24 C (64-75 F) and needs only high humidity, not leaf wetness [S3].
- Infection does not need a long humid spell; humid canopy air at night is often enough; dense planting raises risk [S4].
- Spores germinate when there is free water from rain, dew, fog or irrigation [S5].
- In Florida it is most severe on plants in acidic, sandy soil with high soil moisture [S6].
- For chilli, TNAU notes a dense canopy creates long leaf wetness at night and raises gray mold severity; velvety growth appears under cool humid conditions [S7].

SYMPTOMS
- Light tan or grey spots on leaves that become covered with grey-brown fuzzy growth; stem lesions that can girdle; soft rot and "ghost spots" on fruit [S4][S8].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Keep humidity and leaf wetness low: ventilation, avoid overhead irrigation, rolling up tunnel sides [S1]. In greenhouses keep humidity below 90% [S8].
- Keep adequate calcium; a calcium-to-phosphorus ratio below 2:1 is linked to higher susceptibility [S3]. No tomato varieties are resistant [S1].
- Fungicides are used only after cultural steps; fungicide-resistant strains exist, so rotate groups and follow the label [S1].
- TNAU chilli: remove and burn decaying infected plant parts [S7].
