DOC_ID: 10_fusarium_wilt_tomato
TITLE: Fusarium wilt of tomato (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici)
CROPS: tomato
SENSOR_SIGNALS: temperature (soil) about 27-32 C, optimum about 28 C; low or dry soil moisture; light sandy, acidic soil; alternating cool and warm soil temperature with humidity

FACTS
- TNAU lists favourable conditions as alternating low and high soil temperature with high humidity, optimum temperature 28 C, and light sandy soil, low soil moisture and low pH [S1].
- The South Australian agriculture department also names warm temperatures around 28 C, dry weather and acidic soil, and notes that fast-growing plants fertilised with ammonium nitrate are especially prone; root-knot nematodes raise the chance of infection even in resistant plants [S2]. INRAE likewise gives about 28 C and sandy acidic soils, and says nematode attack or oxygen-poor soil can sensitise plants [S3].
- Symptoms are most common at 27-32 C (80-90 F); the disease develops faster with high nitrogen and low potassium, and in sandy soils [S4].
- The fungus survives in soil or debris for up to ten years (TNAU and INRAE say more than ten) [S1][S3][S4]. It spreads by seed, seedlings raised in infested soil, wind-blown soil, surface water and implements [S1][S2].
- CONTRAST with bacterial wilt (file 09): bacterial wilt is favoured by high soil moisture; sources link Fusarium wilt to low soil moisture and dry weather.

SYMPTOMS
- Yellowing starting on lower leaves, often on one side of the plant or leaf, then leaf curling, browning and drying; brown streaking inside the stem base; stunting [S2][S4].

MANAGEMENT (as stated by sources)
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris [S1]. Clean structures, crates, tools and stakes; stop soil moving between fields on equipment and shoes [S2][S4]. Avoid working in wet plantings [S4].
- Avoid too much nitrogen with low potassium; test soil [S4]. Use seed treatment (TNAU names carbendazim) [S1]. Follow label.
